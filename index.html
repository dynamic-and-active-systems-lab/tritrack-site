TriTrack

TriTrack is a phone app for wildlife VHF radio telemetry. Point the antenna, record the bearing, and see where the animal is while you are still in the field.

What it does

Bearings go straight into a record

Each bearing is saved on the phone as you take it, with the time, your position, the direction, signal strength and uncertainty. Nothing to transcribe at the truck. Tap at the signal’s peak, or sweep across it and let the app take the middle and record the width as your uncertainty. See recording a bearing.

The location, before you leave the field

The phone estimates the animal’s position from the bearings you have taken, with an ellipse for the uncertainty, and warns when the geometry is poor (for example, nearly parallel bearings) while you can still move. Take on map shows a sweep crossing your earlier bearings before you accept it.

No signal needed

Recording and estimating use no network. Download your study area’s map before you leave coverage; the pre-trip checklist shows what is ready. With no server at all, the app is still complete. See how it works.

One dataset for the whole team

With a project server, everyone’s bearings sync into one shared project, with owners, contributors and viewers, instead of several spreadsheets to merge. Teammates’ bearings appear on your map and in your estimates, and syncing happens whenever a connection returns. See projects, members and roles.

It knows which tag was on which animal

Tags, animals and the dates each tag was worn are separate records, so a bearing taken before a tag moved to another animal, or was retuned, stays with the right animal. See managing your tags.

The data comes out as spreadsheets or as a backup of the whole project. See getting your data out.

Where it stands

TriTrack is developed by one person in the Dynamic and Active Systems Lab at Northern Arizona University. It is not available to download yet: there is no store listing or public test version. The guide describes the app as it is today, from an iPhone; nothing here covers Android yet.

If you work with radio-tagged animals and would like to try it on a project, see Support.

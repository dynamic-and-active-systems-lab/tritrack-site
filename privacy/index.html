Most of this policy is not written yet. It will say what TriTrack stores on the device, what leaves it when a project syncs to a server, who can read it, and how long it is kept. Until then, treat nothing here except the section below as a commitment. For an answer sooner, see Support.

Your phone’s own backup, and your records

This part is written because it is the one thing you might need to act on now.

TriTrack’s database is included in your phone’s ordinary automatic backup, on iPhone and Android, like any other app’s files. That is deliberate: a phone lost, drowned or stolen in the field is a likelier way to lose a season than a broken-into cloud account, and the records at risk are the same either way (those not yet synced to your project server). For nearly every project, the backup is worth more than the small extra exposure, and there is nothing to do.

What that exposure is. Apple’s documentation says the keys to an iCloud backup are held in Apple’s data centers, so Apple can read it and can be compelled to produce it. Google does not clearly say whether an app’s files get the extra protection it offers photos and other media, so assume the same of a Google Account backup. This is about the backup, not TriTrack: TriTrack sends your records nowhere except that backup and the project server you choose.

If your project cannot accept that, for example a species whose location must not leak, each phone can turn backup off for TriTrack alone, in the phone’s own settings, without affecting other apps. TriTrack has no setting of its own for this, and will not: the phone’s control already does it, and a second switch invites a false sense of safety.

  • iPhone: in Settings, under the iCloud or Apple Account backup settings, find the per-app list and switch TriTrack off. The wording and place change between iOS versions, so treat this as where to look, not exact steps.
  • Android: Google began rolling out a per-app backup control, in the phone’s backup settings, in June 2026. It has not reached every phone: it has been seen on a Pixel and not yet on a Samsung. If you cannot find a per-app list, your phone probably does not have it yet; you have not missed a step.

What turning it off costs. Anything recorded on that phone and not yet synced then exists only on the phone, and is lost with it. Sync promptly, and before time out of signal take a manual backup file off the phone, which helps whichever way you decide here. See sync and getting your data out.

This page informs a decision your project makes about its own phones. For most projects the right choice is to leave the phone’s backup as it is.

Your phone’s own backup, and your records

This is written, because it is the one thing on this page somebody might need to act on before the rest is ready.

TriTrack keeps its database in the same place every other app on your phone keeps its files, and that means it is included in your phone’s ordinary, automatic backup — the one your phone was already doing before you installed TriTrack, on both iPhone and Android. That is a deliberate choice, not an oversight: a phone lost, dropped in a river or stolen in the field is a far more likely way to lose a field season than a cloud account being broken into, and the records at risk are the same set either way — whichever ones have not reached your project server yet. For nearly every project, having that automatic backup is worth more than the small extra exposure it brings, and there is nothing to do about it.

What being included in the backup actually means. Apple’s own documentation says the keys to an iCloud backup are held in Apple’s data centres, not only on your device — so Apple can read what is in it, and can be made to produce it if legally compelled to. Google does not clearly state whether an individual app’s files are covered by the extra protection it offers photos and other media, so the same should be assumed of a Google Account backup. In both cases this is about the backup, not about TriTrack: nothing about how TriTrack works changes whether your account is ever compromised, and nothing in TriTrack sends your records anywhere beyond that backup and the project server you choose to sync to.

If your project cannot accept that — you are working with a species whose location genuinely cannot afford to leak, and the small risk of a compromised cloud account outweighs the safety net of automatic backup for you — each phone can turn backup off for TriTrack alone, without turning off backup for the whole phone or any other app on it. This is a setting on the phone itself. TriTrack does not have, and will not be getting, a setting of its own for this: the phone’s own control already does the job, one place is better than two, and an app-level switch invites a false sense of safety if somebody forgets to use it.

On iPhone. Look in the Settings app, under the phone’s iCloud or Apple Account backup settings, for a per-app list of what is included in the backup — TriTrack can be switched off there like any other app. The exact wording and location move between iOS versions, so treat this as a description of where to look rather than a set of steps to follow literally.

On Android. Google began rolling this out as a per-app backup control in June 2026, as a setting reached from the phone’s own backup settings, listing apps individually the same way. It has not reached every phone yet — it has been seen on a Pixel and not yet on a Samsung phone, and other manufacturers will land on their own schedule. If you go looking and cannot find a per-app list, that most likely means your phone has not received this control yet, not that you have missed a step.

What turning it off costs you. Anything TriTrack has recorded on that phone and has not yet synced to a project server exists, from that point on, in exactly one place: the phone. A dropped or destroyed phone then loses that work outright, with no backup to fall back on. The remedy is the same one that already protects against a lost phone whatever your backup setting is: sync promptly, and keep the gap between recording a bearing and it reaching the server as short as your signal allows. See the sync page for what the app tells you about records still waiting, and getting your data out for taking a manual backup file off a phone that is about to be out of signal for a while — a different thing from the automatic backup described here, and one that stays useful no matter which way you decide on this page.

This page describes a decision your project can make about your own phones. It does not make that decision for you, and for most projects the right choice is to leave the phone’s own backup exactly as it is.

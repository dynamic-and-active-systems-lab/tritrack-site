Sync

TriTrack records everything on the device first. Sync is what gets it to the server and to your teammates, and gets theirs to you. Nothing you do in the field waits for it.

TriTrack works with no server at all

If you never set up a server connection, the app is complete: projects, tags, bearings, position estimates, the map, exports. There is no bar across the top, nothing tries to reach the network, and nothing nags you about it. A one-person season on one phone is a supported way to use this app, not a degraded one.

Everything on this page applies once a device has been pointed at a project server and somebody has signed in.

The bar

It is on every screen, it is one line, and tapping it opens Sync Status .

In normal use it tells you two things: when this device last managed a sync, and how many records it is still holding. Records waiting is not a problem — it is the ordinary state of a phone that has been out of signal — and the number going down is what a successful sync looks like.

The sync screen

Sync now does it immediately and ignores every setting below. This is the button for the moment you reach the trailhead and want the day’s work off the phone before a long drive.

When it syncs by itself

Four choices, and the sentence under them is the one that matters:

TriTrack syncs while the app is open. It does not sync in the background — while the app is closed, nothing syncs at any of these settings.

So none of these is a timer. They set how long since the last attempt counts as due when you next open the app.

  • Every time I open it — a round trip every time you come back to the app. Most current, most battery and data. Good when you are working beside a colleague on wi-fi and want each other’s bearings now.
  • Every 15 minutes — the default and the one to leave alone.
  • Hourly — fewer round trips, for a metered or satellite plan.
  • Only when I ask — nothing syncs on its own at all. Your records stay on the device until you tap Sync now .

What Only when I ask changes, beyond the obvious. The bar starts naming the setting: instead of just saying how many records are waiting, it says they are waiting because sync is set to only when you ask. That is deliberate — a count of waiting records with nothing about to happen to them is how somebody ends up watching a number that is never going to move.

One thing to know before you leave the default alone. Automatic sync uses whatever connection the phone has, cellular included. There is no wi-fi-only setting yet. On a metered or satellite plan, choose Only when I ask and sync deliberately.

What a failure looks like

The bar changes colour and words. It never goes silent, and it never asks you to do something you cannot do.

Offline

Not a failure. It is the ordinary condition of a phone in a canyon, and the bar deliberately does not shout about it — Offline — last synced , and the date. Everything you record keeps going onto the device. The next round that reaches the server takes it all.

Records the server would not accept

Red, with a number of records refused. The server would not take some of what this device sent. They are still on the device and they are safe — the sync screen says so before it says anything else: These records are safe on this device and nothing has been lost.

Tap the bar. Under Waiting to sync each refused record gets its own card saying which record it is, why it is waiting, and whether there is anything for you to do.

Read the card rather than this page. What it says depends on how much the server explained, and the app is careful about the difference: when the server named the part of the record it objected to, the card names it too; when it did not, the card says so plainly instead of guessing, and it never tells you there is nothing you can do. That wording is being worked on, so this page describes the behaviour rather than reciting the sentence.

The remedy on the card is worth knowing in advance, because it is the one that usually works: look over what the record contains, because text pasted in from somewhere else can carry hidden characters the server will not store . Retype the name or label by hand, save, and tap Try again — an edited record goes back on the wire by itself.

The likeliest reason you will actually see this, and it is fixable in ten seconds: a character in your own name that the server will not store. That is the next section.

Records the server sent that this app could not read

Amber, and it says records did not arrive. A teammate on a newer version of the app wrote something this copy has never heard of — the server sent could not be read by this app.

Nothing has been lost. Those records are safe on the server and on the phone that wrote them. Syncing again will not fix it on its own: update the app, and each of them arrives the next time it changes on the device that wrote it. The sync screen names which records and why.

You are no longer on this project

Grey, and it says you are no longer on this project. You're no longer on this project, so it has stopped syncing. An owner removed you, or you left. Nothing has gone wrong — that is why it is grey and not red. Everything already on the device stays and keeps working offline. See projects, members and roles.

The other three you might see

Amber, and it says the server is refusing access. Usually the same thing as above — but this device cannot confirm it, because the update that would say so is the thing being refused. If you expected to still be on the project, ask an owner.

Amber, and it says you are signed out. Tap it and sign in again. Nothing is lost while you are signed out; records keep piling up on the device and go when you are back.

Red, and it says sync is not working. Repeated failures with no more specific cause. If it persists, whoever runs your project server is the person to ask.

In every one of these the records on your device stay on your device. A failed sync has never lost anybody’s data; it just has not moved it yet.

What the app will accept as your name

This is the one refusal most people will meet, and it is worth knowing before it happens rather than after.

Your first and last name go onto every record you take, and they travel to your teammates’ devices. The app and the server agree on a short list of what a name may be made of, and anything not on the list is refused — not narrowed down from a list of banned characters, but allowed from a list of permitted ones. The list is:

  • letters and the marks that go with them, in every writing system — Latin, Cyrillic, Arabic, Devanagari, Han. Accents, tone marks and the rest are part of the letter, so Đặng Thị and Fernández are ordinary names here.
  • digits, for Bill Gates 3rd
  • hyphens and dashes of every kind
  • spaces of every kind, including the wide space Japanese uses between words
  • apostrophes, for O'Brien, both the straight one and the curly one a phone keyboard produces
  • full stops and commas, for J. R. Smith and Smith, Jr.
  • the middle dot, which is how Japanese writes a foreign name: ジョン・スミス

Everything else is refused, and the list of what that includes is short and surprising: at-signs, brackets, slashes, emoji. Dana Reyes (PhD) is not a name this app will store. That is deliberate rather than an oversight — a name is letters, and the characters people use to decorate one are the same ones used to forge one, by making a name look like an email address on somebody else’s screen.

Where you find out. Since September 2026 the app tells you while you are typing, on the setup screen when you first install it and on your profile afterwards. It does not wait for a sync to fail.

What it tells you depends on whether you can see the character, and this is the useful part:

  • If you can see it — a bracket, an at-sign, an emoji — the message quotes the character back at you and lists what a name may contain. Delete that character.
  • If you cannot see it — a zero-width or formatting character, which is essentially always something that came in on a paste from a web page or a spreadsheet — the message names its code point and tells you to Delete the whole field and type it again . It deliberately does not say that for a visible character, because retyping a bracket you typed on purpose gets you the same refusal.

If it is already saved. A name that was legal when you typed it and is not now is held back at sync rather than lost. Your record stays on your device exactly as it is, the sync screen lists it, and correcting the name on your profile sends it on its own. A teammate whose name is refused is simply absent on your device until they correct it — their bearings are unaffected, because a name and an observation are different records.

The one thing to actually worry about

Records that have never synced exist in exactly one place: that phone. The Sync Status screen says so in as many words — They exist only here until they sync — keep this device backed up until they do.

If you are going to be out of signal for a week with a phone full of the only copy of a fortnight’s bearings, take a backup file off it. That is on the getting your data out page.

What sync cannot do

It cannot run while the app is closed. Not at any setting. A phone in a pack syncs nothing, however good the signal is, until somebody opens the app.

It cannot tell you anything when you are not looking. There are no notifications of any kind. The bar is the whole of the telling and you see it when you next open the app.

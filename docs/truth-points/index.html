Truth points

A truth point is the record of a place somebody physically went to and found the animal, the collar, or both. It is the only record in TriTrack that says where something actually was rather than where the radio suggested it was.

That is what makes it worth the two minutes it takes. Everything else the app produces is an inference from bearings; a truth point is the thing you check those inferences against — how far out was the estimate, and was it out in a way that says something about the terrain, the antenna or the observer.

A truth point never feeds a position estimate. It cannot: TriTrack keeps these records in a different place from bearings, so no calculation can pick one up by accident. If a truth point could reach the solver, the solver would be marking its own homework.

Recording one

Go to the Record tab and pick Truth on the record-type row, then choose the collar on the wheel below it. Two fields then matter more than anything else on the screen.

WHAT WAS FOUND . Three choices, and they are not interchangeable: Animal + collar , Animal only and Collar only . The app says why on the screen — A collar on its own may have been shed. — and the consequence is that only an animal-and-collar point tells you anything about where a live collared animal was. A shed collar in a sagebrush is a fact about the collar.

PRECISION . A radius in metres: how closely you know the spot. This is the one field a truth point cannot be saved without, and the SAVE TRUTH POINT button stays greyed out until it has a number greater than zero in it. A coordinate with no error bar is not a yardstick.

When the phone has a fix, the app fills the precision in for you from the phone’s own reported accuracy and says so, adding Raise or lower it if you know better. — and in the field you very often do. A phone claiming ±12 m while you are standing over the transmitter with the collar in your hand is the normal case, not the odd one. Type over it.

If you place the position by hand on the map instead, nothing is prefilled, and the app explains that too: Not prefilled for a map pin: a pin is only as precise as your ability to identify the exact spot, and the imagery itself is commonly off by a few metres. A pin’s precision is your map-reading, not the phone’s GPS, so the app refuses to put a measured-looking number in a box that did not come from a measurement.

Notes are optional and are the place for what you actually saw — Notes (optional) — e.g. found in burrow . The position row at the bottom behaves exactly as it does for a bearing, including Preview / set position on map for dropping the pin somewhere you can point at but were not standing on. That case is common with a truth point: nobody walks into the middle of a pride to get a GPS fix on a lion.

Where they turn up afterwards

On the map. A truth point is a small square outline in the collar’s colour, with the collar’s name and frequency beside it. Tapping it opens the panel below.

In the Log. Open More , then Log . A truth-point row starts with 📍 Truth point · and the collar, and carries what was found, the precision, who found it and how the position was got, all on the row rather than behind a tap.

In the export. A file of its own. See getting your data out.

The panel on the map

Under the date and the coordinate, three lines, and each answers a question somebody looking at a dot on a map actually has.

  • What was found and the precision, together, because they only mean anything together. Animal-and-collar to ±3 m and a shed collar to ±40 m are different facts and used to render identically.
  • Position: — how the coordinate was got. Four possible answers: GPS fix , Placed by hand on the map , Entered by hand , and GPS fix, later moved by hand . The last one exists because it is genuinely a third thing: calling it a plain fix would claim the phone put it there, and calling it a plain pin would throw away that it started as a measurement.
  • Found by: — the person who recorded it. Not whoever last corrected it; see below.

A point recorded before the app stored these things reads not recorded rather than going blank. That is deliberate: a blank is a gap a reader fills in themselves, and the wrong fill-in here — assuming a GPS fix, or assuming animal-and-collar — is the one that would let an unverified point count towards an accuracy figure.

Navigate on the same panel points the Compass tab at the truth point, with the distance to it, so you can walk back to a spot somebody else found.

Correcting one

Until the end of August 2026 a truth point’s position could not be changed at all. It can now, and anything you read anywhere else that says otherwise is out of date.

There are two ways to open this.

  • From the map. Tap the point, then Edit on the panel.
  • From the Log. The controls on every row live behind the three-dot button at its right-hand end, a truth point’s and a bearing’s alike. The entry reads Edit position / precision / what was found… .

Three things are editable.

  • The position. Move on map… opens the same map picker the record screen uses, and you drag the pin. The case this exists for is the one above: the position recorded was a place near the animal, because you were in a vehicle or on the far side of a fence, and re-recording it from your desk a week later would not improve it.
  • The precision. The field opens focused, because it is the one that gets corrected most. If you have moved the pin the app changes what it says about the number, telling you the figure was recorded for where the point used to be — the old radius described the old spot and is not a claim about the new one.
  • What was found. Same three choices as when you recorded it.

Moving the pin also changes the position line, on its own: a fix you nudge becomes GPS fix, later moved by hand , and a point that never recorded how its position was got becomes an ordinary hand-placed pin rather than claiming to refine a measurement nobody made.

Two things are not editable, and both are deliberate.

  • Who found it. Correcting somebody’s precision figure does not make you the person who walked in and found the collar. Your name is still recorded against the change — it is simply a different fact from the one on the Found by: line.
  • When. The same line the app draws everywhere: when a record was taken is something you re-record, not something you correct.

Who is allowed to

Anyone who can write to the project — a contributor or an owner — can correct or delete any truth point, not only their own. That is a deliberate difference from a bearing, which only its author may edit: a truth point has no author to defer to in the way a bearing’s reading does, and a wrong precision figure on somebody else’s point still spoils everybody’s accuracy check.

A viewer cannot, and the app says which it is rather than failing quietly: Viewers cannot change or delete a truth point — ask an owner to change your role. The roles page has the rest of that picture.

Deleting one

Delete sits next to Edit on the map panel, and is the second entry in the Log row’s menu. It asks first, and the question is worth reading: This is a record of where the animal or collar was actually found — it cannot be re-measured from your desk.

The confirmation that follows carries an undo, and it is a real undo rather than a fresh record — the same point comes back, with its identity, its finder and its history intact. Once you have moved on, the point is gone from the map, the Log and the export, on your device and on everybody else’s once they sync.

What comes out in the export

A project with truth points in it exports three spreadsheets rather than one: the bearings, the no-signal records, and a truth-point file whose name ends in _truthpoints.csv. The message at the end of the export names each kind it wrote, so a kind that is not mentioned means there were none of it rather than that something failed.

The truth-point file has one row per point. Beyond time, latitude and longitude, the columns worth knowing about are:

  • found_kind — animal_and_collar, animal_only or collar_only. This is the column that decides what the row means, and it is the first thing to filter on before computing anything.
  • precision_m — the radius, never empty, because a point cannot be saved without one.
  • position_source — gps_fix, manual_pin, gps_fix_adjusted or manual_entry. Empty only for a point recorded before the app stored it. If you are scoring estimates against these points, this is the column that tells you which of them are measurements and which are somebody’s best reading of a map.
  • found_by — the person, by name. Empty means no finder was ever recorded, and it is never filled in from whoever last edited the row.
  • The tag columns come in pairs, exactly as they do for bearings: what the collar was called and tuned to when the point was taken, and what it is called and tuned to now.

Estimating a position

With two or more bearings on one tag from different places, the Position tab estimates where the animal is, with an ellipse for the uncertainty. It runs on the phone and needs no signal.

Choose the animal

The bar at the top names the animal, its tag and frequency. Tap it, spin to another, and tap Select . An estimate always uses one tag’s bearings.

Choose the bearings

The list holds every bearing on that tag from everyone on the project, newest first: who (initials), when, direction ± uncertainty, strength and quality. A warning triangle means someone ticked Malfunctioning ; it does not remove the bearing.

  • A ticked bearing is in the estimate. Untick one, or tap its ray on the map, and the estimate recalculates.
  • On arrival, bearings within six hours and 50 km of the tag’s newest one are ticked; older or farther ones are listed unticked. All and None tick or untick everything.
  • Unticking lasts for this estimate only. To leave a bearing out of every estimate, an owner uses Exclude from estimates… in the Log; excluded and superseded bearings do not appear here.
  • The sort button orders the list by Time , Member or Distance (needs a GPS fix). Sorting never changes the estimate.

Read the map

Solid rays are in the estimate, faint rays are left out, and each pale wedge is that bearing’s recorded uncertainty. The dot is the estimate and the shaded ellipse its uncertainty; your own position is a green dot.

The ellipse is a 95% confidence region. A long thin one means the bearings fix the animal in one direction only, usually because they came from one side; take the next bearing from across its long axis.

What the ellipse does not catch. It ignores the ± you recorded (that is only drawn), and a bad bearing that roughly agrees with the others is not flagged. In the worked example from the paper the method comes from, one reflected bearing among seven good ones moved the estimate more than five times farther from the truth. Untick a doubtful bearing and watch what changes.

Two bearings give a point with no ellipse, and nothing on screen says so. A position saved from two bearings is listed as 0×0 m; that does not mean exact. Take a third bearing.

The cautions

A caution button in the panel’s top row counts anything worth a second look; tap it for the list. They do not stop you saving the estimate.

  • animal may have moved : the selected bearings span more than six hours (only if you tick older ones yourself).
  • Bearings near-parallel — try a different angle .
  • Poor geometry — try a different observer position : the ellipse is over ten times longer than wide, or reaches more than 5 km out.
  • All observers are nearly at the same point — no triangulation information : all taken within about a meter of each other.
  • Some observations have low-accuracy GPS fixes — reported error may understate true error : at least one position was uncertain by more than 20 m, and the ellipse assumes positions are exact.
  • Solver did not fully converge — treat this estimate as provisional .
  • Only one bearing selected — showing the ray only, no estimate .
  • Geometry too degenerate to estimate — adjust which observations are included : for example, two exactly parallel bearings.

Make room

Drag the handle at the top of the panel up for more list or down for more map. All the way down closes it to a bar; tap the bar to reopen. A caution keeps a warning triangle on the bar.

Save it

SAVE POSITION keeps the location, ellipse, time and the bearings used, and confirms Position saved . It is gray when there is no estimate. A viewer sees Viewers cannot save a position estimate — ask an owner to change your role.

The clock button lists the animal’s saved estimates, each with its ellipse size; delete one with the bin, and UNDO brings it back. Saved estimates also show on the Map tab.

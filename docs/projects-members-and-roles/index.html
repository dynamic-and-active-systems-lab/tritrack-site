Projects, members and roles

Everything TriTrack records belongs to a project: the tags, the bearings, the position estimates, the map extent. One device can hold several, and one of them is active at a time — the one with the green tick in the list.

Tap a project’s name to make it the active one. Everything else is behind the three-dot button at the end of the row, with each entry written out: Publish to server , Members , Edit project , Leave project and, last and separated from the rest, Delete project .

The menu is not the same on every project. Publish to server appears only to an owner, and only on a project that is not on a server yet; Leave project only on one that is. Where the reason is your role rather than the project’s state, the entry is greyed out with the reason written under it rather than taken away — Only a project owner can delete this project. is an answer the menu gives you, not one you have to ask somebody for.

Members is open to everybody on the project, whatever their role, and it opens on a card headed Your role that names yours and says what it lets you do and what it does not. That card is the place to look when something you tried was refused and you cannot tell whether it was your role or a fault; it also names the owners to ask, by name. The table below is the same answer for all three roles at once.

The three roles

Everybody on a project has one of three roles. An owner sets it when they let you in and can change it afterwards.

Three rows in there are worth reading twice.

A contributor cannot take their own bad bearing out of the analysis. If you realise a bearing was wrong, you record a corrected one over the top of it — the original stays on the record and the correction is what counts. Removing a bearing from the analysis entirely is an owner’s decision, because it changes what the whole team sees. Ask an owner if that is what you need.

A truth point is the one record anybody with write rights may correct. A bearing may only be edited by the person who took it; a truth point may be corrected or deleted by any contributor or owner, whoever found it. That is deliberate — a wrong precision figure on somebody else’s truth point spoils everybody’s accuracy check, and the truth points page explains the rest of it.

A viewer really can export everything. The role controls what somebody can change, not what they can read or take a copy of. Anybody you let onto a project at any role can put the whole thing on a memory stick. Choose the role for what you want them to be able to alter; choose the person for what you are willing for them to hold.

Changing a role, and removing somebody

An owner does both from the Members screen, on the row for that person: the dropdown beside their name changes the role, and the icon at the end of the row removes them. The dropdown shows the role’s name on the row and, when you open it, each choice with what it means — Contributor — records their own and its two siblings, the same wording as the sheet you read when you let somebody in.

The app will not let the last owner demote or remove themselves. A project with nobody able to manage it is a project nobody can ever let anyone else into, so make somebody else an owner first.

Removing somebody does not delete them. Their stint moves to Past members with the dates they were on the project, and every record they made stays in the project, still with their name on it. That is deliberate: a permit report or a paper needs to be able to say who collected what and when.

Putting somebody back

A field tech who was on last season’s crew, or somebody who left mid-season and came back, does not have to ask again. On the Past members row there is a button that puts them straight back on — no code, no request, no waiting. They pick it up on their next sync.

You still choose their role and still read the sentence about removal, because it is the same sheet as an approval.

What being removed looks like

To the owner: the person moves to Past members . Before it happens, the app shows the same sentence an owner reads when letting somebody in: removing them stops their app syncing and blocks their access to the server, but it cannot delete what is already on their device.

To the person removed: a grey bar appears across the top of the app saying You're no longer on this project, so it has stopped syncing. It is grey rather than red, because nothing has gone wrong — somebody made a decision. Tapping it opens Sync Status , which says what it means: the copy on their device stays and keeps working offline, and everything they recorded stays in the project for everybody else, still attributed to them.

They will not get a message, an email or a notification. The bar is the whole of the telling, and they see it the next time they open the app.

Leaving a project yourself

Leave project , in the three-dot menu on the project’s row. The app asks first, and says what it will and will not do: you stop syncing, your records stay in the project for everybody else, and the copy on your device stays where it is and keeps working offline.

If the entry is greyed out, the line under it says why. The usual reason is the one above: an owner cannot leave while they are the only owner left, because a project nobody can manage is a project nobody can ever let anyone else into.

To get back on later you can ask to join again with the project code, or an owner can put you back.

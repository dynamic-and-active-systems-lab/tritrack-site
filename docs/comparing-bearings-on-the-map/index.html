Comparing bearings on the map

Two bearings taken from the same spot tell you very little. Two taken from widely separated spots cross somewhere, and that crossing is the animal. The problem is that the map draws every bearing at the same modest length, so the rays you want to compare stop short of each other and you end up extending them in your head.

Since September 2026 you can extend them on the screen instead.

The gesture

Nothing on the map tells you this exists. There is no hint, no tip and no onboarding card — the map has no room for permanent instructions, so this page is the only place it is written down.

  1. Tap a bearing to select it. Tapping anywhere along the ray works, and so does tapping the small circle at its base, which marks the spot the reading was taken from.
  2. A round handle appears at the far tip of that ray. It is there only while a bearing is selected, and it is the whole of the discoverability. If the tip is off the edge of the screen, the handle sits at the last point on the ray you could still reach — so a bearing you have stretched a long way can always be pulled back in.
  3. Press and hold the handle and drag along the line — outwards to lengthen, back towards the base to shorten.

Two honest notes about the pictures above. Every image on this site is generated by driving the real app rather than photographed by hand, so the second one has a marker showing where the finger is; the app draws no such thing. And the bearing in these two happens to be a short one near the top corner, which is where the generated session put it — on your own map the ray you select will usually be longer and more central, and the handle sits at the end of it either way.

Dragging sideways does nothing on purpose. Only the movement along the bearing’s own direction counts, so the tip stays on the line it belongs to and you cannot accidentally swing the bearing to a different heading. The heading is data; the length is not.

While your finger is down, a readout says Drawn to and the distance. That is what makes this a measurement rather than a fidget — you can say how far out the crossing you are looking at is.

Several bearings can be stretched at once. Extend one, let go, extend another, and look at where the two wedges overlap.

The important part: a longer ray is a wider one

This is the thing worth understanding, and it is why the app draws a wedge rather than a line.

Your uncertainty in a bearing is an angle — a few degrees either side of where you pointed. An angle does not shrink with distance. So the further out along the ray you look, the more ground those few degrees cover. A bearing known to within a five-degree cone spans about 35 m of ground at 400 m out, and close to 900 m at 10 km. Same bearing, same confidence, twenty-five times the area.

That is why the wedge fans out as you drag. It is not decoration and it is not the app being pessimistic — it is the honest picture of what a bearing actually tells you at that range, and it is the reason two long wedges crossing at a shallow angle give you a much vaguer answer than the two lines would suggest.

So drag out to see where things cross, and read the crossing off the overlap of the wedges rather than off the point where the centre lines meet.

Lengths are a display choice, not data

A drawn length is never saved, never exported and never synced. It is not part of the observation and it does not reach your teammates. The app says so on the control that resets them:

Press and hold a selected bearing’s tip to drag it longer or shorter. Lengths are a display choice — they are not saved or shared, and they reset when you leave the map.

Two ways back:

  • Double-tap a tip to put that one bearing back to its normal length.
  • Reset bearing lengths , in the Layers & Filters sheet, puts them all back at once. It is greyed out when nothing has been dragged, so a grey entry means there is nothing to reset rather than something being broken.

Leaving the map resets everything anyway.

This is the Main Map only

The rays on the position estimation screen cannot be dragged, and that is deliberate rather than missing. That screen draws its rays at lengths worked out from the estimate it has just computed, so there is nothing to explore — the lengths are already an answer rather than a question. The gesture belongs where you are still working out what the bearings say.

When map tiles stop arriving

Sometimes the background map stops filling in. The app now says so rather than leaving you with a blank grid: New map tiles are not arriving.

It deliberately does not tell you why, because it does not know. A phone with no route out, a field site with no coverage, and the map provider having a bad afternoon all look identical from inside the app, and a confident wrong cause sends you off to fix a network that is fine. What it does tell you is what still works — Tiles already downloaded still draw, and recording a bearing does not use the map service at all.

Recording is never affected. Bearings, positions, notes and truth points do not touch the map service at all.

That message appears on the Main Map, the offline data map, the project extent picker and both maps on the record screen. It does not appear on the position estimation screen — there is no room for it there — so if a map looks wrong on that screen, the Main Map is one tab away and will tell you.

Downloading map tiles for offline use

When you download a region for offline use and some of the tiles do not arrive, the job’s card now says how many rather than quietly reporting a smaller total, and adds one sentence about what to do:

Retry asks only for the ones still missing — whatever did arrive is already on this device.

So a retry is cheap. It never re-downloads what you already have.

Recording a bearing

Pick the tag, point the antenna, take the bearing, save.

The pictures on this page are of a device with no project server, so there is no sync mark on them; the sync page shows what one looks like.

Choose the record type

The icons along the top pick it; only the selected one shows its name. Bearing is a direction to one animal, and most of this page is about it. The others:

  • Omni : heard on an omnidirectional antenna, no direction.
  • Note : something worth writing down here and now that is not a detection.
  • No Signal : you listened for these frequencies here and heard nothing. Record it: it is evidence about where the animal was not.
  • Truth : you found the animal or the tag, and this is where it was. See truth points.

Pick the tag

Spin the wheel to the tag you are hearing. It lists only tags on an animal right now, each as its frequency and the animal’s name. The two-icon switch beside it sorts by frequency or by animal, and the phone remembers your choice.

If the tag you are hearing is not there, scroll to ＋ Add new tag at the bottom of the wheel. It asks for a Tag ID , a Frequency (MHz) and an Animal name , and Adds the tag to the Tag Library and the animal under Animals, wearing it from today. For a tag already in your library, link it instead, under putting a tag on an animal.

With no tag on any animal, the wheel is replaced by No tag is on an animal today (or No tags in this project yet ) and a + Quick-add tag button. A viewer cannot add tags and is told so: Viewers cannot create tags .

Take the bearing

Point the antenna; the number shown is the phone’s live magnetic heading. Then:

  • POINT : tap when the signal peaks. Tap again to clear it.
  • RANGE : hold it while you sweep from one edge of the signal to the other, then let go. The middle of the sweep is the bearing and its width is the uncertainty. Use it on a broad peak; a point tap records a fixed uncertainty instead.

The pencil beside the number types a bearing in by hand, for a hand-compass reading or a notebook. On a device with no compass it reads Manual entry , and the screen says No compass on this device — enter the bearing manually above.

Bearings are stored as magnetic; true north is on the compass tab.

Taking the range on a map

Take on map , the round map button beside RANGE, does the same sweep on a map, so you can see whether it crosses your earlier bearings before you accept it.

It draws today’s earlier bearings on the selected tag, from everyone on the project. Other tags, earlier days, records with no direction, deleted ones, and bearings from before the tag moved to this animal are left out. With no tag selected it says No tag selected, so no earlier bearings are shown.

Hold HOLD TO SWEEP and turn across the signal; the map ignores touches while you hold. Let go and the reading appears, for example 115° ± 15°. A sweep of 180° or more warns that the middle may be the wrong way round. Then:

  • Confirm puts the bearing in the form and closes the map. It does not save. SAVE OBSERVATION still does that.
  • Retake discards the sweep.
  • Cancel (or back) leaves the form as it was.

The button at the bottom right switches North up and Facing up (the map turns with the phone), and the phone remembers which. Dragging the map pauses the turning until you tap Back to me ; if the compass is disturbed the map stops turning and says so. While the map is turned a small north arrow shows; tap it to return to north up. The Terrain / Street / Satellite switch is shared with the Map tab. Like RANGE, the button is hidden on a device with no compass.

Say how good it was

Three wheels under the bearing, one flick each:

  • QUALITY : how much you trust the direction. ✓ Good , Fair , or Bounce? for one you think came off a ridge or a building. Weeks later this tells you which ray to doubt.
  • MODE : which of the tag’s pulse modes you heard. The modes are whatever was named when the tag was entered; the line under the wheels spells out the one picked, with its rate.
  • STRENGTH : how loud, from Faint to V. strong . It is about distance, not direction quality: a faint signal can give a clean bearing and a strong one can bounce.

When the tag itself sounds wrong, for example an irregular pulse, tick Malfunctioning beside the mode line. Bounce? means I do not trust this direction; Malfunctioning means I do not trust this tag. Several on different days are what would justify replacing it. The box does not take the bearing out of estimates; that is done on the Position tab or in the Log.

Where you were

The row above the notes shows the GPS position the record will carry, or that there is no fix yet. Preview/Set shows it on the map and lets you place the pin by hand, for example under a canyon wall.

Notes and Advanced

Notes (optional) is free text. Advanced folds away the bearing method, antenna type and receiver gain, which most people set once a season.

Writing a note

On a Note the text is the record, so the box fills the screen. It carries only the position and time besides, and saves with SAVE NOTE .

Once you type, the box settles to eight lines above the keyboard and the form scrolls. (No picture: the screenshot tool cannot raise a keyboard.) Tapping away keeps every word.

Save it

Tap SAVE OBSERVATION . On a bearing it stays gray until a bearing is taken, which is why it is gray in the picture above. With no position it does not guess: No GPS fix yet — use manual pin instead.

The screen keeps your unsaved entry if you leave and come back. A saved record is on this phone at once; sync sends it to the team when there is a connection.

The compass tab

It is for pointing, not recording: To capture and save a bearing, use the Record tab.

  • LEVEL turns green when the phone is flat enough. A tilted phone reads a wrong heading.
  • True North and Declination need a position fix and show a dash until one arrives. Adjust overrides the declination, for example to match a published value or an older dataset. Recorded bearings stay magnetic either way.
  • ⟲ Calibrate (figure-8) : wave the phone in a figure of eight when the heading drifts or the app warns of interference (a truck, a metal gate, a radio).

On a Mac

A Mac has no compass, so bearings are typed with Manual entry . Everything else is the same. There are no desktop pictures in this guide yet.

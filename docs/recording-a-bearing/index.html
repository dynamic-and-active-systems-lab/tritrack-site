Recording a bearing

This is the thing you will do a hundred times a day. Pick the animal, point the antenna, capture the direction, save.

These two screenshots are of a device with no project server set up, which is why there is no coloured bar across the top. Once a device is syncing there is one; the sync page explains what it says.

Choose what kind of record this is

The row along the top picks it. Directional is a bearing on one animal and it is what the rest of this page is about. The others, briefly:

  • Omni — you heard the animal on an omnidirectional antenna but got no direction from it.
  • Note — something worth writing down at this place and time that is not a detection at all.
  • No Signal — you listened for these frequencies here and heard nothing. It is a real result, not a blank, and it is worth recording: a search that found nothing is evidence about where the animal was not.
  • Truth — you physically found the animal or the collar, and this is where it actually was. It is a different kind of record from all the others and it has a page of its own.

Pick the tag

The wheel under it lists the tags in this project, by frequency, pulse rate and name. Spin it to the one you are listening to.

If the collar you are on is not in the list, + Quick-add tag at the bottom of the wheel adds it there and then with just a name and a frequency, so you are not stopped in the field. A viewer cannot do that and the app says so rather than failing quietly: Viewers cannot create tags .

Take the bearing

Point the antenna. The number under BEARING is the phone’s live magnetic heading. Then one of two buttons:

  • POINT — tap once when the signal is strongest. It latches that heading. Tapping it again clears it and lets you start over.
  • RANGE — hold it down while you sweep from one edge of the signal to the other, and let go. TriTrack takes the middle of the sweep as the bearing and the width of the sweep as how well you know it. Use this when the peak is broad, which is most of the time on a strong signal.

Range is the more honest of the two on a broad peak, because it records your uncertainty instead of hiding it. A point tap assumes a fixed uncertainty.

The pencil beside the number opens a sheet for typing a bearing in by hand — for a reading off a hand compass, or a bearing you wrote in a notebook and are entering later. On a device with no compass at all, that button is labelled Manual entry instead, and the screen says No compass on this device — enter the bearing manually above.

Bearings are magnetic. The compass tab is where true north and declination live, and the next section is about that.

Say how good it was

Three quick pickers under the bearing: BEARING QUALITY , PULSE TYPE and SIGNAL STRENGTH . They take one tap each and they are worth the tap — six weeks later, a bearing marked as a weak, irregular signal is the one you will want to know about when two rays disagree.

Advanced holds the bearing method, the antenna type and the receiver gain, folded away because most people set them once for a season and never touch them again.

Notes (optional) is free text for anything else.

Where you were

Under the notes, the app shows the position it is going to attach to the record. It uses the phone’s GPS. If the fix has not arrived yet it says so, and Preview / set position on map lets you check it on the map, or drop the pin by hand if you know better than the phone does — under a canyon wall, for instance, or when you took the bearing from a spot you can point at on a map.

The position in the picture above is the invented study area these screenshots are generated in, and the number after it is how accurate the phone says that fix is. On your phone it will be wherever you are standing.

Save it

SAVE OBSERVATION at the bottom. For a directional record it stays greyed out until you have actually captured a bearing, so a greyed button means the bearing has not been taken yet rather than something being broken. (That is also why it is grey in the picture above.)

If the phone has no position when you save, it does not save a guess — it says No GPS fix yet — use manual pin instead. and waits for you to place the pin.

You do not have to finish in one go. The line above the button says Draft autosaves , and it means it: leave the screen, take a phone call, come back, and what you had typed is still there.

Saving puts the record on this device. Getting it to your teammates is sync, and it happens on its own when there is a connection. Nothing you record depends on having one.

The compass tab

The dial is for pointing, not for recording — as the screen itself says, To capture and save a bearing, use the Record tab.

The heading in this picture is invented. The screenshots are generated on a simulator, which has no magnetometer, so the app is fed a constant 47 degrees. The dial, the level bubble and the declination arithmetic are the real app; only the number is made up. Do not read anything into it.

Two things on this screen matter in the field:

  • The level bubble. LEVEL at the top right goes green when the phone is flat enough. A tilted phone reads a heading that is wrong by more than you would guess, and this is the cheapest way to avoid it.
  • True North and Declination . The app works out the declination from where you are, so it needs a position fix before it can show a true-north heading; until one arrives both read as a dash. The picture above has one, so it shows both — and because the magnetic heading in it is invented, the true-north figure worked out from it is invented too. The declination is the real value for the invented study area, not for wherever you are. Adjust lets you type a declination in yourself if you have a reason to override it — a published value for the study area, or matching an older dataset. Bearings are stored as magnetic either way, so changing this does not rewrite anything you have already recorded.

⟲ Calibrate (figure-8) is the usual phone-compass fix: wave the phone in a figure of eight. Do it if the heading is drifting or the app warns about interference — near a truck, a metal gate, or a radio.

On a Mac

A Mac has no compass, so the bearing is typed in rather than captured, and the record screen offers Manual entry . Everything else is the same. There are no desktop pictures in this guide yet.
